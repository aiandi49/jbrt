Not From Here

In 1966, an underground musician performing under the name Jimmibeetles recorded a slow, psychedelic version of "Hey Joe" in the New York club scene. A prominent disc jockey, Jack Spector, heard the track and strongly urged him to release it as a commercial single.

Jimmibeetles refused. He believed that commercializing his music would corrupt it. Not long after, the same song became a defining, world-famous hit for Jimi Hendrix. Jimmibeetles never recorded a version of his own.

The Vanishing

Standing firm in that anti-commercial philosophy, Jimmibeetles completely vanished from the music industry in 1971 and remained an absolute recluse for decades. Anyone who witnessed him perform upon what his circle called his "Electric Ark" came away knowing he was, in the words of his own producer, "definitely not from here."

"It took a century for society to discover the works of Johann Sebastian Bach. It has taken over a quarter century to begin to tell the story of Jimmibeetles."

— Zebrax, former producer for Jimmibeetles, 1966–71

Preserving the Legacy

To keep the history from being lost to obscurity, guitarist Billi Pepa formed Jimmibeetles Rock Theater and released The Story of Jimmibeetles in 1996 on the independent label No Records — eleven tracks, several captured live at real New York City venues and moments, not staged after the fact.

One of those moments: a 1969 performance in Central Park during a rainstorm. As the rain fell, the crowd rushed the stage, which was loaded with soaked electrical equipment. Organist Al Beads jammed the highest key on his Hammond and walked away after park attendants forced the show to stop — a recording the album preserves in full, narration and all.

The Story of Jimmibeetles is dedicated to Joseph Paul Tafone (1953–1976),
former road manager and "music brother" of Jimmibeetles.

Where The Wildstarfires Come In

Decades later, The Wildstarfires carry that same lineage forward — the same New York stages, the same refusal to soften the sound for the sake of a hit. In 2026, JBRT became the first inductee into the MetaEarth Music Hall of Fame, with Best of The Wildstarfires Live as the opening chapter of a planned four-album archive.
